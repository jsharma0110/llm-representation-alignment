Conclusion

Directly transplanting a piece of a large language model's internal
representation into a small model's forward pass -- rather than only
training the small model on the large model's outputs -- recovers most of
the accuracy gap between the two models, closing to within 9.4 points of the
large model while running with lower latency than the large model does on
its own.

This suggests that two differently-sized models from the same family share
enough representational structure that a small, targeted bridge can move
real capability across model sizes: not just imitating what the large model
tends to say, but reusing how the large model actually arrived at its
answer.
