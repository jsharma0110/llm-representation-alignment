Introduction

Large language models get more accurate as they get bigger, but bigger models
also cost more to run: more memory, higher latency, higher inference cost per
answer. In practice, teams are often forced to pick one of two bad options: a
small, cheap model that makes more mistakes, or a large, accurate model that
is expensive to serve at scale.

The standard fixes both work by changing the small model's weights: train a
smaller model from scratch, or distill the large model's output behavior into
the small model through ordinary fine-tuning. Either way, the small model has
to re-learn everything from training data alone, and the large model itself
is discarded at answer time.

This project asks a different question: instead of compressing a large
model's knowledge into a small model's weights ahead of time, can we directly
reuse a piece of the large model's own internal computation -- its
intermediate-layer representation of the prompt -- at the moment the small
model needs it most, and hand that representation to the small model to
finish the job?
