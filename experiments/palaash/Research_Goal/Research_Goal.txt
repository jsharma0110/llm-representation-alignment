Research Goal

Recover as much of a large language model's accuracy as possible through a
small model's forward pass, by directly transplanting ("stitching") a piece
of the large model's internal representation into the small model -- rather
than only training the small model on the large model's final outputs.

Specifically: given a small and a large model from the same model family (so
they share a tokenizer and can be compared token-for-token), can a
lightweight trained adapter carry the large model's reading of a prompt into
the small model's later layers, so the small model finishes the answer with
more of the large model's knowledge than it has on its own? And does this get
closer to the large model's accuracy for less cost than simply running the
large model?

This project tests that question on a small and a large model from the same
family (1B and 3B parameters), on a held-out factual question-answering
benchmark chosen specifically because the two models disagree often enough to
measure a real gain.
